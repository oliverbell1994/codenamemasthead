# 01 · Sector playbooks

Sector is the first wizard question because it sets palette logic, density, trust signals, imagery and the exceptions to the house rules. Three sectors are grounded in the founder's own taste; collectibles is provisional (its one build was client-directed); two more are provisional until a build exists.

Each playbook: **Feel · Palette · Type · Density · Imagery · Trust · Exceptions granted · Forbidden here · Language · Accepted reference · Rejected**.

---

## A. Research supplies / peptides (grounded: Buyuk, BPL, Peptidarium brief, Regent)

- **Feel**: clinical, light, precise. Laboratory, not pharmacy; premium supplier, not biotech startup. `[stated]` "peptide businesses should not be dark themed".
- **Palette**: white or near-white paper; cool-tinted panels (ice blue, pale grey-blue); navy ink; one signature (navy-blue on Buyuk; a lab red on Peptidarium `[stated]`). No warm earth tones, no green "wellness" palettes.
- **Type**: Hanken Grotesk + Instrument Sans is the proven pair `[stated]`. Alternatives within the same family of feeling for sister sites that must differ: Host Grotesk, Schibsted Grotesk.
- **Density**: high. Shop page with search, category bar, 3–4 column grid, price, size variants and stock visible without a click. `[stated]` Fewest clicks to checkout. `[stated]`
- **Imagery**: photographic vial renders, correct proportions, consistent cap colour as a brand signature (blue caps on Buyuk, red caps on Peptidarium) `[stated]`. One render per product and size. No drawn vials. `[stated]`
- **Trust**: certificates of analysis per product and batch; purity figures; dispatch service named; WhatsApp/chat button `[stated]`; payment marks; a "scientific team" byline rather than named individuals `[stated]`. No named testing laboratory on the site `[stated]`. No team/people section `[stated]`.
- **Exceptions granted**: none to shape rules. Pure white paper permitted.
- **Forbidden here**: dark themes; green/leaf/wellness imagery; lifestyle photography of people; anything that reads as human-use instruction.
- **Language**: laboratory framing throughout ("research", "in vitro", "laboratory use"). **Never** reconstitution, dosing or mixing guidance; never human-use instructions. `[stated]` Product naming constraints may apply: a product can be sold under a trade name with the compound name in body text only, never in the page title. `[stated]` (GLP-3 / Retatrutide rule.) Consumables (bacteriostatic water, syringes) carry no certificate section. `[stated]`
- **Sister-site rule**: a second site in this sector must differ in layout, content, feel and design from the first while keeping the sector feel. Peptidarium is wiki/library-style with a lab feel; Buyuk is a storefront. `[stated]` The sameness gate is strict within a sector.
- **Accepted reference**: Buyuk "Lumen" direction (white, cool-blue tiles, navy ink, dense shop). BPL as the benchmark for what passes ad review `[stated]`.
- **Rejected**: dark theme; serif headline swap; drawn vial illustrations; circle-B logo; boxy vials.

## B. Beauty / skincare, single-product (grounded: Peptome)

- **Feel**: ultra-high-end cosmetic, credible, distinctive. Not medical (that is sector A), not "clean girl" pastel. `[stated]`
- **Palette**: black, white, greys only. No gold, no copper, no metallic accent. `[stated]` Photography carries the colour.
- **Type**: Hanken Grotesk accepted on the A2 direction `[stated]`. A serif is **not** granted by default; the airy thin-serif direction was rejected as not premium. `[stated]` A heavier serif (≥500) may be offered as one of three directions only.
- **Density**: medium. Split hero (type left, photo right), actives/ingredient grid, a dark product chapter, results section, ingredient ledger, "as seen in" strip. `[stated]` This content flow was accepted.
- **Imagery**: real product photography and video in the hero (video granted `[stated]`); placeholders until the shoot. No AI faces.
- **Trust**: ingredient-led USP (the active named up front, e.g. GHK-Cu as the headline claim `[stated]`); results section; regulatory notification where it exists; "as seen in" strip.
- **Exceptions granted**: a monochrome "frosted glass" buy block (backdrop-filter) `[stated]`; one dark chapter section on a light site `[stated]`.
- **Forbidden here**: metallic accents; medical/lab styling; price or add-to-basket in the homepage hero (visitors go through the product page) `[stated]`; sequential order numbers visible to customers `[stated]`.
- **Language**: ingredient and result claims that are specific and permitted; no "miracle", no medical claims. Logo and wordmark are locked. `[stated]`
- **Accepted reference**: Peptome "option 1 → A2" direction.
- **Rejected**: dark/cinematic direction; lab-blue direction; airy thin serif; gold/copper accents; the original Lovable-era design.

## C. Collectibles / trading cards (provisional — Card Castle was client-directed, not founder taste)

**Correction, 3 Nov 2026:** on Card Castle the client locked exactly two things: the blue background and the logo. `[stated]` Everything else (gradient gold buttons with icons, the icon strip, pills) was the founder's own call *within* that locked navy, because the flat version "looked a bit plain" on a dark ground. `[stated]` The founder dislikes the overall result ("I hate the blue"). So: the navy and the logo are client locks; the richer treatment is a **contextual** decision (what to do when a client locks a dark ground), not a sector rule and not a house rule. The playbook below is the founder's own default for this sector.

- **Feel**: premium hobby store with real product authority: the feel of a good watch or vinyl dealer, not a gaming site. `[inferred]`
- **Palette**: house rules apply. Near-black or deep warm ink on paper, one signature drawn from the shop's mark; no corporate blue; dark only if the owner's locked brand requires it. `[inferred]`
- **Type**: refined grotesk per 00; a weight-500+ serif may be offered as one direction for a heritage/dealer feel. `[inferred]`
- **Density**: medium-high; product grid with set, condition, grading and price; express-delivery promise prominent. `[stated]` (the delivery promise was in the brief)
- **Imagery**: product photography of sealed product and singles on neutral ground; the owner's mark used as given, never redrawn unless unlocked.
- **Trust**: physical store address and hours; delivery promise; grading/condition language; payment marks.
- **Exceptions granted**: none by default. **Contextual rule from Card Castle**: when a client locks a dark ground, a flat minimal treatment reads as plain; use the signature colour structurally (buttons, an icon strip) to give it weight. Pills and gradients remain outside the house style and are added only if the owner asks, with the engine saying so in one line.
- **Forbidden here**: generic "shop now" heroes; gaming-site neon; rounded card grids.
- **Language**: enthusiast-literate (set names, print runs, grading terms) without hype.
- **Accepted reference**: none yet from founder taste. Card Castle stands as a client-locked build the founder would not choose.
- **What the founder said that still applies**: keep a client's layout when they like it and make it less generated-looking without changing it much `[stated]`; the founder's own bar for this sector is the same as everywhere else, Mesh and Buyuk `[stated]`.

## D. Crypto / AI products (grounded: Mesh, Solvane)

- **Feel**: clean, simple, "tier-1". `[stated]` Think exchange or infrastructure company, not meme site.
- **Palette**: may be dark; near-black with one restrained signature. Never neon-on-dark, never purple-blue. `[inferred]` from rejections.
- **Type**: Host Grotesk accepted `[stated]`; Geist and Bricolage rejected as AI-common `[stated]`. No oversized display headings. `[stated]`
- **Density**: low-medium; fewer, stronger sections; data and numbers shown plainly.
- **Imagery**: product UI screenshots, diagrams of the mechanism, one ambient animation behind the hero. `[stated]` No orbs, no floating coins, no glowing nodes.
- **Trust**: contract addresses, audits, live figures, named chains and partners.
- **Exceptions granted**: ambient hero animation `[stated]`; dark theme permitted.
- **Forbidden here**: three-step icon cards `[stated]`; generated-looking logos `[stated]`; "light editorial" treatments (rejected as basic `[stated]`).
- **Sister-site rule**: a relaunch must look completely different (different fonts) while staying premium. `[stated]`
- **Accepted reference**: Mesh (the founder's bar for "T1" in this sector `[stated]`).
- **Rejected**: first Solvane live site (logo, three-step cards, hero all judged AI/basic); light editorial first design.

## E. Wellness / supplements, multi-product (provisional)

- **Feel**: premium, calm, credible; closer to B than A. Not green-and-leafy.
- **Palette**: warm neutrals, one muted signature; no metallics unless the brand is positioned luxury.
- **Type**: grotesk lead; a weight-500+ serif may be offered as one direction.
- **Density**: medium; catalogue grid with benefit tags (square, not pills).
- **Imagery**: product photography on neutral ground; ingredient macro photography.
- **Trust**: ingredient sourcing, third-party testing, subscription honesty.
- **Forbidden here**: leaf icons, "natural" clichés, pastel gradients, stock yoga photography.
- To be grounded when the founder's wellness brand is built.

## F. Clinics and service businesses (provisional)

- **Feel**: calm authority. Light, structured, with the practitioner and the place visible.
- **Palette**: white paper, one deep signature (navy, forest, oxblood), warm greys.
- **Type**: grotesk lead; serif granted at weight ≥500 for a heritage positioning.
- **Density**: low-medium; services as a hairline-ruled list with prices where permitted; booking action always visible.
- **Imagery**: real premises and real people, photographed; never stock smiles.
- **Trust**: registrations, accreditations, named practitioners with credentials, reviews pulled from a verifiable source only.
- **Forbidden here**: stock photography, "book now" pill buttons, icon grids of services.
- To be grounded with the first clinic customer.

## Choosing a sector when the fit is unclear

Ask which existing sector the customer's buyers would recognise, not which the owner identifies with. A supplement sold on efficacy claims is E; sold on laboratory purity, it is A; sold on beauty results, it is B. When two fit, generate directions from both and let the owner choose; the chosen direction fixes the sector for the rest of the build.
